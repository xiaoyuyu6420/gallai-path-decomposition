\section{A conditional route: Gallai modulo explicit hypotheses}\label{sec:conditional}

Several proofs in the literature --- Lov\'asz' deletion--reinsertion, Fan's star absorption,
and the analysis of minimal counterexamples --- fail at the same type of point: one needs a
decomposition of a reduced graph in which \emph{specified vertices hold sufficiently many path
endpoints}, and the count of available endpoints is not controlled by the usual hypotheses.
This section sets up a framework measuring exactly that, proves its basic lemma, and shows that
a single budget conjecture $K$ implies Gallai's conjecture outright; a reduction chain then
shows how two further conjectures would settle the hardest residual layer of $K$. The section
is deliberately conditional: nothing here assumes Gallai's conjecture. Its central deliverable
is the \emph{explicit hypothesis ledger} of Section~\ref{subsec:ledger} and the master
conditional statement of Section~\ref{subsec:modulo}: every reduction edge is stated with its
verification level, its premises and its residual gap, so that every conditional claim in the
paper is checkable against exactly the premises it needs --- at full self-contained depth for
$\Pi1$--$\Pi5$, and at registered-provenance depth for the three bridge conjuncts
$\Pi6$--$\Pi8$, whose archive-only definitions are flagged at their entry points
(Section~\ref{subsec:ledger}).

\subsection{The absorption-cost lemma}\label{subsec:absorb}

\begin{lemma}[absorption cost]\label{lem:T1}
Let $G$ be a graph, $D$ a path decomposition of $G$, $v$ a vertex not incident with any edge of
$G$ ($v$ may be a new vertex), and $N=\{w_1,\dots,w_d\}\subseteq V(G)$ a set of $d$ distinct
vertices; let $G^+=G+\{vw: w\in N\}$. Build the bipartite graph $B(D)$ with left side the $d$
edges $vw$ of the star and right side the paths of $D$, where $vw$ is adjacent to $P$ iff $P$
has $w$ (or $v$) as an endpoint; let $\mu^*$ be a maximum matching of $B(D)$. Within the
following operation class --- (i) attach a star edge $vw$ to the $w$-end of a path of $D$
ending at $w$; (ii) pair two star edges $vw,vw'$ into the single path $w$--$v$--$w'$; (iii) let
a star edge be a single-edge path --- the minimum number of new paths required to extend $D$ to
a decomposition of $G^+$ is exactly $\ceil{(d-\mu^*)/2}$, and the resulting $D(v)$ can be made
at least $\mu^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\le$) Match $\mu^*$ star edges to distinct paths, each attached at the matched endpoint as in
(i); each matched path absorbs one star edge and remains a path. The remaining $d-\mu^*$ star
edges are paired into $w$--$v$--$w'$ paths ($w\ne w'$, $v$ appearing once), with at most one
left over as a single-edge path: $\ceil{(d-\mu^*)/2}$ new paths.

($\ge$) In any sequence of operations (i)--(iii), the star edges absorbed by old paths (operation
(i)) correspond to a matching of $B(D)$: a single path cannot absorb two star edges, since two
attachments at its two ends would close a cycle through $v$. The unabsorbed star edges are
covered by new paths, each of which contains at most two of them. Hence the number of new paths
is at least $\ceil{(d-\mu^*)/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:T1scope}
Lemma~\ref{lem:T1} is an equality within its operation class; it is \emph{not} a lower bound
for arbitrary decompositions of $G^+$, and arguments that use it as an impossibility proof must
declare the operation class. Three obstruction phenomena in the literature are instances of
``the cost of Lemma~\ref{lem:T1} exceeds the budget'': the Lov\'asz-style vertex reinsertion
gap (the unproved strengthening of Theorem~\ref{thm:lovasz} to path-only decompositions with
endpoint control), the parity wall for even-sized stars in the Fan engine (a star of even size
leaves its centre even, so no endpoint at the centre is forced, and $\mu^*$ may fall $k$ short),
and the simplicity wall of Fan's Lemma~\ref{lem:fan6} (a host endpoint may exist but be
unusable because the path through it already contains the reinsertion target). The same wall
resurfaces in Remark~\ref{rem:gap}. The following two elementary facts quantify when the budget
suffices.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:buried}
Let $D$ be a decomposition of $G$ with $|D|=B$, and let $x$ be an even-degree vertex.
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em]
\item If $\deg_G(x)=2B$ then $D(x)=0$; in general $\beta(G,x)\ge\deg_G(x)/2+1$, where
$\beta(G,x)$ is defined below.
\item If $|D|=\mathrm{odd}(G)/2$ then $D(x)=0$ for every even-degree vertex $x$.
\item $\beta(G,x)\le\pn(G)+1$ for every even-degree non-isolated $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\sum_{P\in D}\deg_P(x)=\deg(x)$ with $\deg_P(x)\le2$; if $|D|=B=\deg(x)/2$ then every
path contains $x$ internally, so $D(x)=0$. For the general claim, let $D$ be any decomposition
with $D(x)\ge2$ and let $t\le|D|$ be the number of paths containing $x$; at least two of these
meet $x$ as an endpoint, contributing $1$ each, so $\deg(x)=\sum_P\deg_P(x)\le 2t-2 < 2t \le
2|D|$, hence $|D|>\deg(x)/2$ and, the values being integers, $\beta(G,x)\ge\deg(x)/2+1$.
(ii) The total number of path-ends is $2|D|=\mathrm{odd}(G)$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:LB1}(ii)
every odd vertex consumes at least one, so all are consumed and even vertices get none.
(iii) Take an optimal decomposition; if $D(x)\ge2$ we are done, and otherwise $x$ is internal
to some path $P$ (it has positive even degree); cutting $P$ at $x$ produces $\pn(G)+1$ paths
with $D(x)=2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The budget conjecture and its strength}\label{subsec:budgetK}

The endpoint budget previewed in Section~\ref{subsec:machnotation} is formalized as follows.

\begin{definition}\label{def:beta}
For a connected graph $G$ and an even-degree non-isolated vertex $x$, let
\[
\beta(G,x)\;:=\;\min\{\,|D|\ : D \text{ a path decomposition of } G,\ D(x)\ge2\,\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[$K$]\label{conj:K}
For every connected graph $G$ on $n$ vertices and every even-degree non-isolated vertex $x$,
$\beta(G,x)\le\ceil{n/2}$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:Kgallai}
Conjecture~$\ref{conj:K}$ implies Gallai's conjecture: every connected $n$-vertex graph $G$
has $\pn(G)\le\ceil{n/2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $G$ has an even-degree non-isolated vertex $x$, then $K$ provides a decomposition with at
most $\ceil{n/2}$ paths and we are done. Otherwise $G$ is all-odd, so $n$ is even, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:lovasz} gives a decomposition into $p$ paths and $c$ cycles with $p+c\le
n/2$. In a mixed path-cycle decomposition the parity identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:LB1}(iii)
still holds (cycles contribute even degree at each vertex), so every vertex is an endpoint of
at least one path and $2p\ge n$; hence $p\ge n/2$, so $p=n/2$, $c=0$, and the decomposition is
pure, giving $\pn(G)\le n/2=\ceil{n/2}$.
\end{proof}

Thus $K$ is a \emph{strengthening} of Gallai's conjecture, not a consequence of it: any proof
of $K$ must overcome the full difficulty of Gallai's conjecture on graphs with even-degree
vertices, plus the endpoint requirement. We record the evidence for $K$.

\begin{remark}[evidence for $K$]\label{rem:Kevidence}
Conjecture~$K$ was verified with zero violations on: all connected graphs on $n\le7$ vertices
($996$ graphs, the OEIS A001349 cumulative through $n=7$; the archived atlas summary printed
its domain as $956$ graphs / $3{,}238$ even-vertex instances --- a domain-total discrepancy of
the same registered bookkeeping kind as the $n=9$ count note of Appendix~\ref{app:comp},
flagged for re-verification; the zero-violation verdict reads on the run's enumerated domain);
all connected graphs on $n=8$ vertices with at
most $13$ edges ($4271$ graphs, $16746$ even-vertex instances); $400$ random connected graphs
with $n\in\{8,9,10\}$, $m\le13$; $22$ structured families (stars, complete graphs, wheels,
butterflies, complete bipartite graphs, cycles, double stars, cocktail-party graphs, trees up
to $n=100$); and a stress family of $\ceil{n/2}$-tight graphs with pendant leaves. In the
hardest regime, $\pn(G)=\ceil{n/2}$, the value $\beta(G,x)=\ceil{n/2}$ is attained with
equality throughout (never exceeded), and Proposition~\ref{prop:buried}(i)--(ii) explains the
mechanism in the tight instances found. All computations used the exact pipeline of
Appendix~\ref{app:comp}. Two cautionary negatives from the same study: the analogous bound for
\emph{odd} vertices is false, and the $\floor{n/2}$-level version $\beta\le\floor{n/2}$ is
false (stars, wheels and equality-tight graphs violate it); $\ceil{n/2}$ is the correct level.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Tight odd-order graphs and the reduction chain}\label{subsec:tightreduction}

Call a connected graph $H$ on $N$ vertices \emph{tight} if $\pn(H)=\ceil{N/2}$. Tight graphs
are exactly the hard case for Gallai's conjecture and for $K$ (by
Proposition~\ref{prop:buried}(iii), $K$ can only fail at a tight graph). We need two
statements about them; the first is a two-line equivalence.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:Nequiv}
Let $k\ge1$ and let $H$ be a connected graph on $2k+1$ vertices. Then
$|E(H)|\ge2k^2+1$ if and only if $H$ is an odd semi-clique (Definition~\ref{def:osc}).
Consequently, the statement ``every tight connected graph on $2k+1$ vertices is an odd
semi-clique'' is equivalent to ``every connected graph on $2k+1$ vertices with
$|E|\le2k^2$ satisfies $\pn\le k$''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$|E(H)|\le\binom{2k+1}{2}=2k^2+k$, so $|E|\ge2k^2+1$ iff the complement has at most
$2k^2+k-(2k^2+1)=k-1$ edges, which says exactly that $H$ is an odd semi-clique. The second
statement is its contrapositive form.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[density of tight odd-order graphs]\label{conj:N}
For every $k\ge4$, every tight connected graph on $2k+1$ vertices is an odd semi-clique.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[pair-hosting for odd semi-cliques; SC-PH]\label{conj:SCPH}
Let $H$ be an odd semi-clique on $2k+1$ vertices and let $w_1\ne w_2$ be two non-isolated
vertices of $H$. Then $H$ has a path decomposition with at most $k+1$ paths in which $w_1$ and
$w_2$ are endpoints of two distinct paths.
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:SCPH} attributes to the odd semi-cliques the minimal hosting property that
Conjecture~$K$ would force on tight graphs; it is the natural semi-clique analogue of
Question~1.1 of \cite{BP19} with endpoints specified. The following theorem says that the two
conjectures jointly settle the one remaining obstruction in the tight layer of $K$'s
degree-two analysis: the possibility that two specified vertices are always forced to share a
host path (a ``fail pair'').

\begin{theorem}[reduction chain]\label{thm:reduction}
Assume Conjectures~\ref{conj:N} and \ref{conj:SCPH}. Let $H$ be a tight connected graph on
$2k+1$ vertices and let $w_1\ne w_2$ be non-isolated vertices of $H$. Then $H$ has a path
decomposition with at most $k+1$ paths in which $w_1$ and $w_2$ are endpoints of distinct
paths.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $k\le3$: an exhaustive computational census of all connected graphs on $3,5,7$ vertices ($2$, $21$, $853$
graphs respectively, each computed exactly) shows that the tight ones have $2k^2+1\le|E|\le2k^2+k$,
i.e.\ are odd semi-cliques (a computational fact; Appendix~\ref{app:comp}), so Conjecture~\ref{conj:SCPH} applies
directly. If $k\ge4$: since $H$ is tight, Conjecture~\ref{conj:N} makes $H$ an odd
semi-clique (the density equivalence being recorded in
Proposition~\ref{prop:Nequiv}), and Conjecture~\ref{conj:SCPH} applies. In both cases $H$ admits a decomposition
into at most $k+1$ paths hosting $w_1,w_2$ on distinct paths.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} should be read as a map of what remains. In the inductive analysis
of Conjecture~$K$ for an even vertex $x$ of degree $2$ (delete $x$, decompose $G-x$, reinsert
the two star edges by Lemma~\ref{lem:T1}), every case is settled except one: when $G-x$ is
tight on an odd number of vertices and its optimal decompositions host the two neighbours of
$x$ only on a common path. Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} removes exactly this case, conditionally
on Conjectures~\ref{conj:N} and \ref{conj:SCPH}, in the tight odd-order layer that arises after
deleting $x$; the remaining work is (a) a proof of SC-PH, i.e.\ an explicit endpoint-assigned
construction for odd semi-cliques, and (b) Conjecture~\ref{conj:N}, of which the evidence is:
true with proof for $k\le3$; certified for $k=4$ by an edge-stratified machine adjudication
of the connected graphs on $9$ vertices (zero counterexamples; the accepted census total for
connected graphs on $9$ vertices is $261\,080$, OEIS A001349, while the archived pipeline
records a domain of $210\,864$ --- a bookkeeping discrepancy analysed in
Appendix~\ref{app:comp}); supported by a directed
search at $k=5$ ($164$ candidates near the density threshold, zero counterexamples); and
reinforced by a barrier theorem --- any counterexample must simultaneously escape nine
published classes (Pyber, Fan, $\Delta\le5$, treewidth $\le4$, $2$-degenerate, planar, block
graphs, $\EV$ complete, triangle-distance) and have a tightness unexplained by any standard
computable lower bound. By contrast, on \emph{even} orders tight graphs not explained by any
lower bound abound ($365$ found at order $8$ alone), which is what makes the odd-order
statement plausible and the even-order analogue false.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The $L4$-with-Inf supply side: a closure candidate}\label{subsec:l4inf}

The $G1$ entry of the grid below inherits the ``$L4$-with-Inf paper side open''
obligation and the $G5$ card rests on the same layer; the wave-26 pass registers a
closure candidate for the with-Inf supply half, machine-certified on the full
with-Inf cube and passed at an adversarial replay with notes. Write $S\subseteq R$
with $\mathrm{Inf}\in S$, $F:=S\cap\mathbb{Z}_{16}$, $m:=|F|$, $n=m+1$; let
$A_\sigma(F)$ ($\sigma\in\{0,1\}$) count the adjacent pairs of $F$ on parity
$\sigma$, and $d(F)$ half the number of full antipodal pairs
$\{x,x+8\}\subseteq F$:

\begin{proposition}[$L4$-Inf: the supply side at the with-Inf sets; candidate, not
a theorem]\label{prop:l4inf}
\emph{(a) Closed forms.} For every such $S$ and every $c\in F$,
\[
\mathrm{cap}_S(c)=m-A_{c\bmod2}(F),\qquad
\mathrm{cap}_S(\mathrm{Inf})=m-d(F),
\]
hence $\mathrm{maxcap\_avail}(S)=m-\min\bigl(d(F),\ \min_{c\in F}
A_{c\bmod2}(F)\bigr)$.
\emph{(b) Supply bound.} If $2\le m\le14$ (i.e.\ $n\in[3,15]$), then
$\mathrm{maxcap\_avail}(S)\ge\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil$, and the bound is attained for
every $n\in[3,15]$.
\emph{(c) Boundaries.} $n=2$: minimum $1$; $n=16,17$: minimum $8=k$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof structure.} The two closed forms are proved by a cancellation: the
$[c{+}1\in F]$ bracket of the pure-finite formula is exactly offset by the finite
neighbour's hit on the $\mathrm{Inf}$ class $c\bmod 8$ --- the $n=9$ repair law
registered at erratum C1 (Appendix~\ref{app:errata}), here generalized to every $m$
and proved. Three geometric facts carry the bound: no nonempty proper circular
interval of $\mathbb{Z}_{16}$ is $+8$-invariant; a circular interval of length $L$
carries exactly $\max(0,L-8)$ full antipodal pairs; and the adjacent-pair count
equals $m$ minus the number of runs. The parity split then excludes equality: at
odd $m$ the extremal antipodal count forces a single run, whose antipodal count
cannot equal the odd target for $m\le13$; at even $m$ it forces $F=F+8$, the runs
pairing as $\{I,\,I{+}8\}$, whence $\min(A_0,A_1)\le j-1$ for $m=2j$.

\emph{Calibre carried (erratum E1, previewed as errata group~O).} The bound of (b)
is stated at $\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil$ (equivalently $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$); the
source report also prints the same constant as $\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1$, an identity
false at even $n$ (at $n=4$ it reads $2$, not $3$) --- a statement-layer slip
registered by the replay, with the theorem content, both proof branches, sharpness

erratum row is previewed in Appendix~\ref{app:errata}.

\section{Concluding remarks and open problems}\label{sec:open}

This section collects what the paper leaves open, in two registers. The
\emph{paper-side} problems O1--O8 are the open questions of the mathematical
chapters, ordered roughly by judged accessibility. The \emph{campaign-side}
register R1--R19 is the machine adjudication's own list of residual openings ---
honestly registered open items, not defects --- transcribed here in one-line
form; the verbatim register, together with a per-item status refresh checked
against the current frontier and the wave manifests, is carried in
Appendix~\ref{app:finalverdict-open}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item \textbf{The remaining columns of Case~4} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:27A}). Is
$\pn(K_{n_1,n_2})=\ceil{n_1/2}$ for all odd $n_1$ and even $n_2$, $2\le n_2<n_1$? The
conjecture now has the shape of a finite column-by-column programme
(Section~\ref{subsec:27a}): the general cap (Theorem~\ref{thm:capgen}), the general structure
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:structgen}) and ring closure (Theorem~\ref{thm:ringclosure}) reduce
the column $n_2=2h$ to $h+1$ explicit base instances $k\in[h,2h]$, the tail $k\ge2h+1$ being
free. The columns $n_2=2,4,6$ are closed; the open bases are $K_{15,8},K_{17,8}$ (column
$n_2=8$) and $K_{17,10},K_{19,10},K_{21,10}$ (column $n_2=10$) --- the five named base

burnt more than $14$ million nodes ($n_2=8$) and about $0.9$ million nodes ($n_2=10$), over
every ledger-feasible deficit multiset, without a hit and without exhausting a single schedule

missing decompositions lie deeper in the search tree, or the conjecture fails --- in the
latter branch the deficit multisets $(0,8,0)$ (at $K_{15,8}$) and $(1,8,0)$ (at $K_{17,10}$)

$8\le\pn(K_{15,8})\le11$. The next methodological step suggested by the data is a
constraint-propagation search driven by the deficit ledger of Theorem~\ref{thm:structgen} and
the $B$-endpoint parity, rather than deeper node budgets.
\item \textbf{Removing the structural conditions} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:Astar}). With the
alignment and separation hypotheses of Theorems~\ref{thm:Astar} and~\ref{thm:sep} both
dropped, the star-deletion argument fails exactly at \emph{remote high vertices}
(Remark~\ref{rem:gap}); by Proposition~\ref{prop:residue}, a minimal counterexample must have
such a bad partner at \emph{every} high vertex. The two-high layer is closed except the corner
of Corollary~\ref{cor:twohigh}: empty on the constructed family proper whenever the counting
gap is at most $2$ (the band-free tightness material of Remark~\ref{rem:gap} and
Appendix~\ref{app:band}), and for larger gaps blocked only by the excess bound
$\mathrm{excess}\le\lfloor y/2\rfloor$ (zero violations on all $235$ computed instances;
provable $\pn(\tilde G)\le\mathrm{odd}(\tilde G)/2+2B-1$ for $B$ bands, exactly $+1$ for a
single band). Outside the constructed family, far--far edges and multiple parity leaves can be
admitted, while cycle-bearing odd-neighbour blocks are genuine obstructions --- the first
known counterexamples to the general band-free statement. The residue of the conjecture is
this corner (at gap $\ge3$ within the family) together with the graphs of at least three high
vertices that are neither separated nor covered by the profile parity of Remark~\ref{rem:CO};
any full proof must import endpoint-budget ideas
(Section~\ref{sec:conditional}) into the Fan engine.
\item \textbf{The path-partition problem of the deadlock chain}
(Section~\ref{subsec:deadlock}). The residual open statement there is a pure path-partition
problem: is the static layer of the chain always feasible, i.e.\ does the residual edge set
always partition into pairwise edge-disjoint simple paths with at most
$b(v):=t(v)-\mathrm{deaths}(v)$ of them ending at each vertex $v$? It is supported at model
level by $215{,}251$ in-hypothesis instances with zero failures, settled on components of at
most two cycles with a demand source present and on single cycles with pendant trees under the
per-vertex anchor condition, and the pendant-cycle counterexamples recorded in
Section~\ref{subsec:deadlock} show that a forest residual, while always sufficient, is
genuinely unavailable in general; the cut-feasibility equivalence suggests flow-type
formulations of the endpoint budgets as the natural entry point.
\item \textbf{Tightness in the unbounded regime.} In random searches restricted to graphs
satisfying (A1)--(A2) with $\Delta(\EV)\ge5$, no instance attaining $\pn=\floor{n/2}$ was
found. Is $\floor{n/2}$ simply not sharp for Conjecture~\ref{conj:Astar}'s class, i.e.\ is
$\pn\le\floor{n/2}-1$ true there beyond the parity-forced exceptions?
\item \textbf{SC-PH and the density of tight odd-order graphs}
(Conjectures~\ref{conj:SCPH}, \ref{conj:N}). By Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} these two
together settle the residual tight layer of $K$'s degree-two analysis. SC-PH asks for an
explicit endpoint-assigned decomposition of odd semi-cliques; the natural approach combines
the Walecki cycle decomposition with the placement of the $\le k-1$ deleted edges and the two
specified hosts. The $k=4$ computational layer of Conjecture~\ref{conj:N} carries the
domain-shortfall flag registered in Section~\ref{subsec:ledger}.
\item \textbf{The degree-$\ge4$ layer of $K$.} Beyond degree $2$ the deletion--reinsertion
scheme of Lemma~\ref{lem:T1} meets its true wall (Remark~\ref{rem:T1scope}); the
computational record shows the wall is an artefact of the method, not of $K$. What is needed
is a construction that produces a $\ceil{n/2}$-decomposition with $D(x)\ge2$ directly,
without deleting $x$.
\item \textbf{The even-$j$ Walecki residual.} Theorem~\ref{thm:L0} is for odd $j$. For even
$j$, $K_{2j+1}-P_0$ decomposes into $j$ paths for all tested $j\le5$ (chains containing
$K_5$/$K_9$ blocks), and the general case would extend Theorem~\ref{thm:chain} to all
odd-order complete-block chains. The natural route is a Hamilton decomposition of
$K_{2j+1}-C^*$ for a suitable cycle $C^*$ through the prescribed path, which touches the
Oberwolfach problem for odd orders.
\item \textbf{The $4$-degenerate residual.} The theorem of \cite{ZLH24} covers
$3$-degenerate graphs; the class of $4$-degenerate graphs is, as far as we know, entirely
open. $K_{4,n}$ shows the interesting behaviour starts immediately: it is $4$-degenerate and,
by Theorem~\ref{thm:k4odd}, beats its published bounds by one.
